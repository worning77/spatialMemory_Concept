Concept, as a flow

Plain-language version of the concept in its current logical order (2026-09-21): from Gutwin and Greenberg to five questions, five mechanisms and the features that realize them. Replaces the August concept page, which followed the old order. The study design that hangs off this flow is on the Plan tab and the shared sheet.

The flow in one line: two of the three ways people learn what others did are gone once the other person has left; a note said at the thing brings one of them back, a released body trace brings back the other; five mechanisms keep what came back true as the room changes; the features are just how those five show.

1Where the past goes (Gutwin and Greenberg)

Face to face you learn what others did three ways: you watch them work, you hear them talk, and you read the state of the things they touched. In a room used in shifts the actor is gone, so the first two are cut and only the third is left, and a thing's state tells you the present, not the past. A nudged camera rig looks calibrated.

What the later person needs is the past half of workspace awareness. Gutwin and Greenberg list six past-tense questions; we group them by their question word into five: where has a person been, when did that happen, what has a person been doing, who was here, and how did this come to be (two of the six are how-questions). The grouping is ours; the questions are theirs.

2The base: a note said at the thing (authored feedthrough)

Standing at the thing, you say one sentence. No recipient, no description of where, because the place supplies both. The note is pinned there and the next person who walks up sees it. The content is what you meant to say; the delivery is the place. This is the medium's base promise, the origin of everything below, and it is not a mechanism.

The four-step loop lives here. Capture (one breath, anywhere: a note said while walking lands on the thing it names, else where it was said, else at the nearest station with a flag). Anchor (the place). Structure (the mechanisms of section 3). Surface (a dot from afar, the card when you approach, several cards fanning out at one anchor).

Two kinds of memory, decided at capture by what a note is anchored to and how long it lives. Object memory is anchored to a place or object and outlives any project ("0.6 nozzle is in" on the printer). Project memory is anchored to a project, follows its work across places, and dies with it ("set the cameras to 40"). Person and time are not kinds of memory but queries over the graph.

3Five things that make a room hard, one mechanism each

Each row is one of the five question words, turned into a knob the room can turn up. Each mechanism keeps that question answerable as the knob rises. The rows are independent by construction; the shared sheet's crossings table argues that no mechanism helps on another row.

The fifth row is where the second channel enters. No note can answer it, because the point is that nobody wrote one. So the first severed channel, watching bodies work, is recorded instead: record (inertial odometry between stops, a short camera look when the wearer stops), release (the wearer's decision at the end of the session), segment (stops become presence at anchors, walks become transits past things), surface (the same graph). The trace knows who passed, not what happened. Speaking is consent by itself; the trace needs an explicit release.

4The Memory Graph

Nodes: anchors (built once from a room scan), cards (made live from speech), people. Edges: supersedes, project, depends_on, audience, author, present_at, passed_near, plus replies_to and confirmed_by, which are kept but not studied. Queries: notes_at, thread, trail, recent, for_me, who_passed_here. Gathering, collecting what one action needs from several places, is what the queries do over the edges the mechanisms made; it has no mechanism of its own. On arrival the queries compose: the room hands over the few items that matter today.

5Features, by mechanism

Everything runs live. Kind is written as category: object. The build rule: nothing outside the origin and the five mechanisms gets built; replies_to, confirmed_by, expiry and urgency stay as one sentence each in the concept.

The whole system is four parts: one graph, two inputs (speech to cards, body to presence), one renderer with a handful of card states, six queries. The second input is what makes it bigger than a notes app, and it is the technical spine.

6AnchorNote and us

Same capture, different everything else. AnchorNote (Hundiwala and Monroy-Hernández, arXiv 2603.20199, 2026) does what our origin does: speech, live transcription, an LLM summary, a note anchored in the room and shared across headsets. It is synchronous pair brainstorming on Snap Spectacles, N = 20. It has no structure over the notes, no asynchronous use, no second channel, no awareness framing, and cameras always on. We take the capture as a known move and cite it; our claim starts where theirs ends: sequential occupancy, the five mechanisms, the trace, and glasses that open the camera only when the wearer stops.
