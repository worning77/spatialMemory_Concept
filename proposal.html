Proposal

The paper in five blocks, each with what it says, the decisions behind it, and the questions still open. Written 2026-09-21 on the assumption that the system runs live on glasses. The concept flow is on the Concept tab; the study derivation is on the shared sheet.

In one line: a room used in shifts carries its own past on two channels, what people said in place and where their bodies went, kept true by five mechanisms, on glasses that open the camera only when the wearer stops; a week-long scenario shows it runs, and a study against Slack shows arrivers get a correct picture.

1Intro

Says. People share workspaces without sharing time. Of the three ways to learn what others did (watch them, hear them, read the state of things), the first two are cut once the actor leaves, and the third shows the present, not the past. Leaving word costs the leaver everything and pays them nothing, so the small facts die. Spatial Memory lets the room carry its past: a note said at the thing appears at the thing; a released trace says who was here; a Memory Graph keeps both true. Built on glasses without a continuous camera. Shown in a week-long scenario, tested against Slack.

Decided. Contributions are four: the concept (two channels and the graph as awareness maintenance), the system on glasses, the scenario, the study plus a pipeline benchmark. One positioning sentence against AR annotation and AsyncReality. A "what we did" sentence with scale numbers before the contribution list. Writing follows AsyncReality's structure.

Open. The exact pairing for the positioning sentence (AsyncReality, or the AR-annotation class), decided after the positioning table is drawn.

2Concept

Says. Gutwin and Greenberg's six past-tense questions, grouped by question word into five (where, when, what, who, how). The base: a note said at the thing, with the four-step loop (capture anywhere, anchor, structure, surface) and the two kinds of memory (object memory outlives projects, project memory dies with its project). Five things make a room hard, one mechanism each: stacking, Supersede; divergence, Route; crowding, Audience; voices, Authorship; silence, Trace. The fifth is where the second channel enters: no note can answer it, so the body is recorded and released by its owner. Then the graph: nodes, edges, three queries (notes_at, collect along an edge, for_me); gathering is what queries do, not a mechanism.

Decided. Dimensions are defined from the situation alone (how many distinct X the arriver's question involves) and only then matched to mechanisms; the crossings table proves each mechanism answers one dimension and no other. Anchoring is the medium itself, not a mechanism. Unrecorded change is reinstated as the fifth dimension because Trace gives it a mechanism and it takes the fifth question word. Edges are material, mechanisms are behaviour; replies_to, confirmed_by, expiry and urgency stay as one sentence each. Privacy: speaking is consent, the trace needs an explicit release.

Open. Whether the section reveals the two channels up front or at the fifth mechanism (current choice: at the fifth). Length: the material is five pages, AsyncReality's concept is two.

3Scenario

Says (not rewritten yet). A week in the maker lab: Sam seeds on Monday; Renata supersedes, the box moves, the watch logs on Tuesday; Iris fails on the nudged camera on Wednesday, the boundary of the spoken channel; the trace catches Dae-ho's transit on Thursday, the cure-time card is carried and read first; letting go on Friday.

Decided. The five-day plot survives. What changes: Thursday becomes the trace catch instead of a repair by a person; one walk-and-talk beat is added (the blocked aisle); every beat is shot on the running system, the storyboard illustrations are placeholders; a beat-to-mechanism table closes the section. The scenario carries "it runs"; the study carries "people get the picture".

Open. Whether Friday's letting-go beat stays now that expiry is not built. Whether the rig facts (Kalibr, WiLoR, Anipose at 40 fps) still hold for the lab as staged.

4System

Says. Four parts. One graph (anchors from a one-time room scan, cards, people; seven edges). Two inputs: speech to cards (wake word, live STT, an LLM that proposes the segmentation and the anchor, constrained to the room's anchors, confirmed on the spot) and body to presence (inertial odometry between stops, a short camera look at each stop against the scan, smoothing at session end, station segmentation into present_at and passed_near, release control). Three queries. One renderer: dot afar, card on approach, fan-out, fold, carried, dimmed, author chip; floor ribbon, on-object count, replay; placement by rule (anchor plus offset, facing the viewer, stacked until approached), not by optimization. An assumptions subsection first: static fixtures, one-time scan, the wearer's own devices, moving objects out of scope.

Decided. Everything runs live; "offline" is retired except for the benchmark. Odometry (InrtlAR v7) and relocalization (hloc or AprilTags) are cited components, not evaluated. Technical content is one pipeline benchmark table (segmentation, anchor proposal, filing, audience and urgency, links, correction rate, against two ground truths) and one trace-validity paragraph (station attribution, dwell error, walk-and-talk anchor hits, camera-on fraction counted from stops). No ablations, no comparison tables. Build rule: nothing outside the origin and the five mechanisms.

Open. Platform: RayNeo glasses (the paper as written) or Quest (the trace shrinks to a feature and the paper becomes concept plus study). First check whether the RayNeo SDK can keep its own SLAM off and deliver frames on demand. Power on the glasses cannot be measured; the camera-on fraction is counted, not measured, and the paper says so. The seeding of the study room is done by speaking through the live pipeline, then curated.

5Study

Says. Two roles, two studies. Leaver: a small Wizard-of-Oz seeding study (4 to 6 people, 30 to 40 minutes of real lab work per condition, speak in place versus phone and Slack; capture is real, confirmation is simulated, no AR display needed) that yields notes per hour, words per note, time off task, four-element completeness and TLX, and doubles as the seed corpus and the benchmark corpus. Arriver: between-subjects, Spatial Memory versus Slack (the same notes as channel messages on a phone, headset worn either way), 8 plus 8 growing to 12 plus 12, on the corner tasks derived from the sheet: arrival, floor, pile, shared bench, route, attribution, the nudged camera, with resume and trail as one room under two prompts; a leave-word coda with a real release decision. Measures: action against a scripted truth, time, verify-before-act, the three trust probes (fold, filter, signature), TLX and preference; findings per task.

Decided. Positioning: the study measures whether the arriver's picture is correct (awareness), not whether the tool saves effort; PaperToPlace is the contrast case. Tasks come from the five dimensions at their poles, one mechanism each, with the construction rule that without the mechanism the information is unreachable; the crossings table's partials are the guard list for staging. Statistics at N = 12: proportions per task, RM-ANOVA or ART with effect sizes for time and TLX. The August single-arm design is reversed because nine of eleven UIST precedents carry an everyday baseline in the study.

Open. Re-alignment with YY and Darren on the Slack arm. IRB: whether the design change needs a modification. Whether a stale-top-card probe goes into the pile task. Between-subjects as planned, or within-subjects with two matched wings of the lab if pilots show small effects.

6Venue and timeline

UIST 2027 first (early April 2027), ISMAR 2027 as the nine-page fallback (about mid-March 2027), CHI 2028 if the study slips. Order of work: platform decision; Nymeria simulation of the trace (station attribution versus fixes per minute); YY and Darren; IRB; build the graph and the two inputs; seed the room through the live pipeline; pilots; run; write.
